Before you start

In 2005, Malaysia sent more students to Australia than all but three countries. Where do you think it ranks in 2025?

Almost three times as many students, but fewer Malaysians

In 2005, Australia hosted about 289,000 international students. By 2024 it had a record 851,000. The climb was not smooth: a slide after 2009, a long boom in the 2010s, and a sharp fall when Australia closed its borders during COVID-19. Malaysians went the other way: fewer study in Australia today than in 2005.

While the total nearly tripled, the number of Malaysians fell from 18,074 to 14,895. It peaked at 26,003 in 2018 and has stayed well below that level since COVID-19.

Students or enrolments? A student is one person. An enrolment is one course. One student can hold several enrolments, for example an English course followed by a degree. So enrolment totals (about 1.05 million in 2025) are higher than student totals (844,000). The tag above each chart title says which one it counts.

Just over half of international enrolments are at universities (Higher Education). About a third are vocational courses (VET).

English-language courses (ELICOS) are a smaller slice, but they swing the most. They are usually the first course a student takes, so they react first when borders or visa settings change.

Malaysia shrank while the market almost tripled. So who moved ahead of it?

Ten countries have overtaken Malaysia

China and India have led throughout. Behind them, ten countries have moved past Malaysia since 2005, led by Nepal, Vietnam and the Philippines. Malaysia was a top-five source every year from 2005 to 2018; it is now 13th.

The Philippines and Bangladesh grew just as fast from a smaller base. Malaysia's share fell even though Australia as a whole grew, and South Korea followed the same path.

Raw totals favour big countries. Dividing by population tells a different story: Bhutan sends 1,635 students per 100,000 people, about 40 times Malaysia's rate. For Malaysia, about 41 people in every 100,000 are studying in Australia: above Indonesia (9) and India (10), but far below Singapore (113) and Nepal (237).

Totals count everyone already studying. To see whether the slide will continue, look at who is starting.

Malaysia missed the post-COVID rebound

New students are the pipeline: each year's starters become the next few years' totals. Most large source countries had their biggest intake in 2022 or 2023, after the borders reopened, then fell in 2024 and 2025. Malaysia's intake peaked much earlier, in 2017 (8,189), reached only about half that in 2023, and was 3,116 in 2025: 38% of its peak. With fewer starting each year, the number of Malaysians studying in Australia has kept falling.

For the Malaysians who still come, where in Australia do they study?

Two inner cities hold four in ten enrolments

New South Wales and Victoria have hosted about two-thirds of international students for twenty years. Within them, students cluster in a handful of inner-city areas. Malaysians lean even more towards Melbourne: almost half of them study in Victoria.

And what do they come to study?

What they study depends on where they come from

Most Chinese and Bangladeshi students are at university. Most Filipino, Colombian and Brazilian students take vocational or English courses. Malaysians sit in between: just over half at university and almost 40% in vocational courses.

What this means for Malaysians

So how did Malaysia go from 4th to 13th? Three things happened together: the market almost tripled, ten faster-growing countries moved past it, and fewer Malaysians started courses after 2017.

  1. 13thBusier, but fewer Malaysians. Australia hosts almost three times as many international students as in 2005, yet fewer Malaysians than it did then. Malaysia was a top-five source every year to 2018 and is 13th today.
  2. 4×A new mix of classmates. Since 2015, students from Bangladesh, Nepal and the Philippines have each grown more than fourfold, and South Asian countries are now among the fastest-growing sources.
  3. 48%Melbourne is the Malaysian choice. Almost half of Malaysians study in Victoria, against 30% of all international students. Outside the capital cities, regional areas hold under 7% of all enrolments.
  4. 26%Generalists, not specialists. Business is the top field for Malaysian university students, at 26%, but their mix across fields is the most even of the 12 countries shown.

About this visualisation

Who it is for
Malaysians thinking about studying in Australia, their families, and anyone curious why Malaysia has slipped down the list of source countries.
Why
International education is one of Australia's largest export industries. This page shows how Australia's international student population grew and changed over 20 years, and where Malaysia fits.
What it shows
Where students come from, how many arrive each year, where in Australia they study, and what they study, from 2005 to 2025.
How to read it
Five parts, each with a headline that states the finding. Red always means Malaysia. Hover over any chart for exact values; use the slider or Play on the flow map.

Method notes

  • “Students” are a December head count of unique students. “Enrolments” count courses and are higher.
  • Rates are students per 100,000 home-country residents (world map) and per 1,000 state residents (Australia map).
  • Three very small territories with no map shape in Natural Earth 1:110m are left off the world maps but kept in the totals.
  • Map projections: Equal Earth (world, centred on the Pacific) and Albers conic equal-area (Australia), so area comparisons are fair.
  • Flow lines are curved and thin flows are drawn above thick ones to reduce overlaps, following Jenny et al. (2018), Design principles for origin-destination flow maps.
